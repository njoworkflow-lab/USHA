02 · Why a brain, not a chatbot

A chatbot read about it. A brain lived it.

General AI knows what the books say. It has never raised a price on a Monday and watched the wholesalers’ orders on Thursday. It has never sat through a credit committee cutting its ramp-up forecast in half. Experience is the part you can’t download.

Pricing · FMCG, EgyptIllustrative example

A chatbot says

“Price elasticity for snacks is typically between −1.5 and −2.5. Consider a moderate increase.”

A brain says

“Don’t cross the EGP 10 point. On the last three launches I ran, moving from 10 to 12 lost a third of volume in six weeks. Hold 10, take the pack to 38 g, and tell your top 20 wholesalers before a competitor does.”

Feasibility · Hospitality, KSAIllustrative example

A chatbot says

“Build TAM, SAM and SOM, then project occupancy growth over five years.”

A brain says

“Your ramp-up is the weak line. Mid-scale hotels in secondary Saudi cities took about 30 months, not 18, to reach 65% occupancy in the projects I reviewed. Rebuild the cash curve on 30 and see if the bank still says yes.”

HR · Family group, UAEIllustrative example

A chatbot says

“Use structured interviews and a competitive compensation package.”

A brain says

“Your new COO leaves in month nine if they haven’t signed a single decision by month six. I’ve watched it in four family groups. Put their approval limits in the offer letter, not in a promise.”

  • Pattern memory. “I’ve seen this before, and I know how it ended.”
  • Hand-made judgement. Which number in the model is the lie. Learned by getting it wrong once.
  • Situations faced. The supplier who vanishes in Ramadan. The forecast that’s always 30% high in year two.
  • Then the evidence. Every view is checked against current data and research before it reaches you.

04 · How a brain is made

Built with the consultant. Signed by the consultant.

  1. Step 1

    Consent

    The consultant signs first: what their brain covers, what it never touches, and their right to pause it. Nothing is captured before that.

  2. Step 2

    Capture

    Weeks of structured interviews on real past cases, with client names removed: the situation, the options, the call they made, what happened, what they’d do now. Their first questions, their red flags, their rules of thumb.

  3. Step 3

    Validation

    The consultant tests the brain on cases they choose. They write their own answer first, then score the brain’s: same verdict, same numbers, same risks, same first move. We correct and re-test until they sign it off.

  4. Step 4

    Licence

    The consultant licenses the brain to USHA and earns a share of every subscription, every month it runs.

Every profile shows the brain’s version and validation date.

08 · Questions

Before you consult.

Is this the consultant in person?

No. It’s a validated replica of their judgement: their cases, decisions and rules of thumb, built with their authorisation. The consultant tested it and signed it off. Their CV is on LinkedIn if you want the person for a larger project.

How close is it to the real consultant?

Each consultant tests their brain on their own past cases and scores it against the answers they’d give. It goes live only after they sign it off. Each profile shows the version and the date it was validated.

What do I get each month?

The brain on your WhatsApp or Telegram for the month. Ask as often as the project needs, within fair use, for one company and one project brief. You can update the brief each month.

How is the price set?

By three things: the market rate for this consultant where they practise, their years of experience and how many markets they cover. Prices run from $300 to $4,000 a month.

Can I speak to the human consultant?

Their CV is on LinkedIn and you can contact them there. Any direct engagement is between you and them.

What won’t a brain advise on?

Legal, tax, political and medical questions. When your case touches one, the brain says what it means for the business and which licensed professional to bring in, then carries on with the business decision.

Is my case confidential?

Your brief and your conversation are used only to answer you. They aren’t used to build or improve other brains, and the consultant doesn’t read your conversation unless you invite them.

How do I pay?

Not online. Reserve your consultation; the team contacts you on WhatsApp or Telegram to confirm the brief, agree payment and activate it.

Can I stop or switch?

Yes. Subscriptions are monthly. Stop at the end of any month, or move to another brain from the next month.

How is this different from USHA?

USHA runs your marketing every day. A brain gives you one senior expert’s judgement on one area of the business. USHA is the host: she reads your case and introduces the right brain.
